## Are they distinguishable on accuracy?

Both models answer the same 246 questions, so the paired comparison is the one that counts: the questions both get right or both get wrong say nothing about the difference between them, and the evidence is entirely in the questions exactly one of them gets right. Approach 1 has one set of predictions (its seeds are identical), so it is paired against each transformer seed in turn. The test is McNemar's, exact rather than approximated because the counts are small.

| Approach 2 seed | Both right | Only Approach 1 right | Only Approach 2 right | Both wrong | Approach 1 minus Approach 2 | 95% interval on the difference | Exact p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 42 | 218 | 11 | 8 | 9 | +1.2 points | -2.3 points to +4.7 points | 0.65 |
| 43 | 216 | 13 | 6 | 11 | +2.8 points | -0.6 points to +6.3 points | 0.17 |
| 44 | 215 | 14 | 7 | 10 | +2.8 points | -0.8 points to +6.5 points | 0.19 |

The rule was fixed before the numbers were seen: the two approaches are called distinguishable only when every seed's paired test is below p < 0.05 with the same approach ahead each time, indistinguishable when none is, and mixed otherwise.

**On accuracy, the two approaches are statistically indistinguishable on this test set.** The 95% intervals on the two accuracies overlap, and the paired test does not reach p < 0.05 for any of the 3 transformer seeds (p between 0.17 and 0.65). The mean gap of +2.3 points in Approach 1's favour is inside the noise of a test set this size. What separates the two is cost, not correctness: Approach 1 trains in 1.6 s against 15 min 22 s for Approach 2, a factor of 588, with 293 times fewer parameters.
